\section{What Does Robust Steerability Tell Us?}
\label{sec:theory}

We now give a unified interpretation of robust steerability.  The result below
shows that the score identifies the most consequential disturbance direction,
certifies its attenuation, places LQR and $\mathrm{H}_{\infty}$ steering on a
single robustness path, and quantifies when the certificate transfers to the
nonlinear transformer.  Proofs are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

Let
$\statevar{w}=\operatorname{col}(\wk)_{k=1}^{L}$ and
$\statevar{z}=\operatorname{col}(\zk)_{k=1}^{L}$.  For a controller
$\steervar{K}$, denote by $\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}$ the lifted linear map
such that
$\statevar{z}=\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}\statevar{w}$, and let
$\steervar{K}^{(\gamma)}$ be the controller returned by the fixed-$\gamma$
backward recursion.  We write $\steervar{K}^{\mathrm{LQR}}$ for the
finite-horizon LQR controller with the same state, control channel, quadratic
weights, and terminal cost.

\begin{theorem}[Robust-steering path and local certificate]
\label{thm:unified-robust-steering}
Assume $R_k\succ0$ and that the standard finite-horizon full-state
$\mathrm{H}_{\infty}$ recursion is used.  Let $\gamma^\star$ be its smallest
feasible attenuation level.  Then:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)] \textbf{Exact linear meaning.}
    For every admissible controller $\steervar{K}$,
    $$
    \steervar{\gamma}(\steervar{K})
    =
    \norm{\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}}_2
    =
    \sigma_{\max}(\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}).
    $$
    Hence, a worst unit-energy disturbance is any principal right singular
    vector of $\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}$.  Moreover, for every
    $\gamma>\gamma^\star$, the controller
    $\steervar{K}^{(\gamma)}$ satisfies
    $$
    \norm{\statevar{z}}_2
    \leq
    \gamma\norm{\statevar{w}}_2
    $$
    for every disturbance sequence entering through $\{\Dk\}$, with
    $\statevar{x}_1=0$.  If the optimum is attained, then
    $$
    \gamma^\star
    =
    \inf_{\steervar{K}\in\steervar{\mathcal K}}
    \norm{\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}}_2
    =
    \frac{1}{\Srob}.
    $$

    \item[(ii)] \textbf{Nominal-to-robust continuation.}
    For every layer $k$,
    $$
    \Kk^{(\gamma)}
    =
    \Kk^{\mathrm{LQR}}
    +
    \mathcal{O}(\gamma^{-2}),
    \qquad
    \gamma\rightarrow\infty.
    $$
    Thus, LQR and near-optimal $\mathrm{H}_{\infty}$ steering are the nominal
    and robust endpoints of the same finite-horizon Riccati family.

    \item[(iii)] \textbf{Local nonlinear transfer.}
    Suppose that, whenever $\norm{\xk}_2\leq\rho$,
    $$
    \norm{\statevar{r}_k(\xk,-\Kk\xk)}_2
    \leq
    \steervar{c}_k(\Kk)\norm{\xk}_2^2,
    $$
    and define
    $\bar c_{\steervar{K}}=\max_k\steervar{c}_k(\Kk)$ and
    $\eta_\rho(\steervar{K})=\rho\bar c_{\steervar{K}}$.
    Let $\mathcal{G}_{xw}$ and $\mathcal{G}_{zw}$ be the linear closed-loop
    maps from $\statevar{w}$ to the stacked state and regulated output, and let
    $\mathcal{G}_{xr}$ and $\mathcal{G}_{zr}$ be the corresponding maps from an
    additive transition error $\statevar{r}$.  If
    $$
    \norm{\mathcal{G}_{xr}}_2
    \eta_\rho(\steervar{K})
    <1,
    $$
    then, with
    $$
    \Delta_\rho(\steervar{K})
    =
    \frac{
        \norm{\mathcal{G}_{zr}}_2
        \eta_\rho(\steervar{K})
        \norm{\mathcal{G}_{xw}}_2
    }{
        1-
        \norm{\mathcal{G}_{xr}}_2
        \eta_\rho(\steervar{K})
    },
    $$
    every disturbance small enough to keep the trajectory in the
    radius-$\rho$ neighborhood then satisfies
    $$
    \norm{\statevar{z}_{\mathrm{nl}}}_2
    \leq
    \left[
    \steervar{\gamma}(\steervar{K})
    +\Delta_\rho(\steervar{K})
    \right]
    \norm{\statevar{w}}_2.
    $$
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Part (i) gives $\Srob$ an exact operational meaning: it is controlled by
the disturbance sequence that is most amplified through depth toward the
regulated semantic outputs, not necessarily by the largest residual in
Euclidean norm.  A large residual may be suppressed by later layers, whereas a
smaller but behaviorally aligned residual may determine the certificate.

Part (ii) strengthens the comparison with A-LQR.  The two controllers are not
isolated alternatives; they belong to a common family indexed by
$$
\rho_{\mathrm{rob}}
=
\frac{\gamma^\star}{\gamma}
\in[0,1),
$$
where $\rho_{\mathrm{rob}}=0$ is the LQR limit and
$\rho_{\mathrm{rob}}\uparrow1$ approaches the strongest feasible worst-case
attenuation.  This is the finite-horizon analogue of the classical
minimum-entropy connection between $\mathrm{H}_2$ and
$\mathrm{H}_{\infty}$ control
\citep{doyle1989state,iglesias1990discrete}.  The theorem does not imply that
downstream LLM metrics vary monotonically along this path; rather, it predicts
that stronger distribution shifts should favor more robust operating points.

Part (iii) states precisely when the linear score can remain informative for
the original transformer.  Transfer requires both a local small-gain margin
and coverage of realized mismatch by the calibrated channels $\{\Dk\}$.
The curvature constant is controller- and placement-dependent: post-block
affine interventions add no current-block curvature, whereas pre- and
intra-block interventions can shrink the validity region.  Explicit placement
constants are given in Appendix~\ref{app:intervention-placement}; both local
conditions are evaluated in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.
