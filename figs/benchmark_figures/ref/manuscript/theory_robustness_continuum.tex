\subsection{Nominal and Robust Steering Form a Continuum}
\label{sec:robustness-continuum}

The attenuation level $\gamma$ is not only a feasibility certificate; it also
indexes a family of controllers between nominal and worst-case steering.  For
any $\gamma>\gamma^\star$, let
$\steervar{K}^{(\gamma)}=\{\Kk^{(\gamma)}\}_{k=1}^{L}$ denote the controller
returned by the fixed-$\gamma$ backward game recursion.  Let
$\steervar{K}^{\mathrm{LQR}}$ be the finite-horizon LQR controller constructed
from the same $\{\Ak,\Bk,Q_k,R_k\}$ and terminal cost, but without an
adversarial disturbance.

The discrete-time finite-horizon disturbance-attenuation recursion and its
qualitative reduction to the ordinary linear regulator as
$\gamma\to\infty$ are classical
\citep{basar1991dynamic,hassibi1999indefinite}. The next result starts from
that recursion and records the quantitative, layer-uniform specialization
needed here.

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative finite-horizon LQR limit]
\label{prop:lqr-limit}
Suppose the horizon is finite,
$Q_k\succeq0$, $Q_f\succeq0$, and $R_k\succeq rI$ for some $r>0$.
Assume that the system and cost matrices are finite-dimensional, bounded, and
independent of $\gamma$. Starting from the standard fixed-$\gamma$ minimax
Riccati recursion of \citet{basar1991dynamic} (see also
\citet[Ch.~9]{hassibi1999indefinite}), there exist a finite threshold
$\Gamma$ and finite constants $C_k^P,C_k^K$, independent of $\gamma$, such
that the recursion is feasible for every $\gamma\geq\Gamma$ and
$$
\norm{P_k^{(\gamma)}-P_k^{\mathrm{LQR}}}_2
\leq
\frac{C_k^P}{\gamma^2},
\qquad
\norm{\Kk^{(\gamma)}-\Kk^{\mathrm{LQR}}}_2
\leq
\frac{C_k^K}{\gamma^2}
$$
for every layer $k$.  In particular,
$P_k^{(\gamma)}\to P_k^{\mathrm{LQR}}$ and
$\Kk^{(\gamma)}\to\Kk^{\mathrm{LQR}}$ at rate
$\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{-2})$. Thus, this bound quantitatively refines the
classical qualitative $\mathrm{H}_{\infty}$-to-LQR limit for the present
finite-horizon, time-varying formulation.
\end{proposition}

This result suggests the normalized robustness coordinate
$$
\rho
=
\frac{\gamma^\star}{\gamma}
\in[0,1),
$$
where $\rho=0$ is the LQR limit and $\rho\uparrow1$ approaches the smallest
feasible attenuation level.  Intermediate values trade nominal efficiency for
greater protection against calibrated model mismatch.  This continuation is
closely related to classical minimum-entropy $\mathrm{H}_{\infty}$ control,
which connects $\mathrm{H}_2$ and worst-case synthesis
\citep{doyle1989state,iglesias1990discrete,peters1995connections}.
Importantly, the proposition does
not assert that downstream task metrics vary monotonically with $\rho$; which
point on the path is preferred is an empirical property of the deployment
shift.

The same framework also permits accumulated-error feedback through a standard
state augmentation.  Because transformer depth is finite, we interpret this
extension as correction of persistent layerwise bias rather than a literal
zero-steady-state guarantee; its formulation and ablation are deferred to
Appendix~\ref{app:integral-steering}.

\section{Robustness Continuation and Integral Augmentation}
\label{app:robustness-continuum}

\subsection{Fixed-$\gamma$ backward recursion}

We take as our starting point the standard dynamic-game solution of the
finite-horizon discrete-time disturbance-attenuation problem
\citep{basar1991dynamic,hassibi1999indefinite}. Specialized to our notation,
its fixed-$\gamma$ backward recursion is as follows.

For notational clarity, write $P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}$ for the value matrix at
layer $k+1$ and define
$$
M_k^{(\gamma)}
=
\gamma^2 I
-
\Dk^\top P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}\Dk.
$$
Whenever $M_k^{(\gamma)}\succ0$, maximizing over the disturbance yields the
robustified value matrix
$$
\widehat P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}
=
P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}
+
P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}\Dk
\left(M_k^{(\gamma)}\right)^{-1}
\Dk^\top P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}.
$$
The controller and value recursion are
$$
\Kk^{(\gamma)}
=
\left(
R_k+\Bk^\top\widehat P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}\Bk
\right)^{-1}
\Bk^\top\widehat P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}\Ak,
$$
$$
P_k^{(\gamma)}
=
Q_k
+
\Ak^\top\widehat P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}\Ak
-
\Ak^\top\widehat P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}\Bk
\left(
R_k+\Bk^\top\widehat P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}\Bk
\right)^{-1}
\Bk^\top\widehat P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}\Ak.
$$

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:lqr-limit}]
Theorems~3.1 and~4.1 of \citet{basar1991dynamic} give the normalized
finite-horizon dynamic-game recursion above; the general $R_k\succ0$ form
follows by the standard input change of coordinates
$\tilde u_k=R_k^{1/2}u_k$ and is also given in
\citet[Ch.~9]{hassibi1999indefinite}. We therefore take this recursion as the
starting point. The classical argument observes qualitatively that the game
reduces to the linear regulator when $\gamma\to\infty$
\citep[proof of Thm.~5.2]{basar1991dynamic}. We strengthen that observation by
bounding the perturbation uniformly over the finite horizon.

Define the LQR gain and Riccati maps
$$
\mathscr K_k(X)
=
\left(R_k+\Bk^\top X\Bk\right)^{-1}\Bk^\top X\Ak,
$$
$$
\mathscr R_k(X)
=
Q_k+\Ak^\top X\Ak
-\Ak^\top X\Bk
\left(R_k+\Bk^\top X\Bk\right)^{-1}
\Bk^\top X\Ak.
$$
Thus
$\Kk^{\mathrm{LQR}}=\mathscr K_k(P_{k+1}^{\mathrm{LQR}})$ and
$P_k^{\mathrm{LQR}}=\mathscr R_k(P_{k+1}^{\mathrm{LQR}})$.
For the robust recursion, define
$$
\mathscr H_{k,\gamma}(P)
=
P+P\Dk
\left(\gamma^2I-\Dk^\top P\Dk\right)^{-1}
\Dk^\top P.
$$
Then
$$
\Kk^{(\gamma)}
=
\mathscr K_k\!\left(
\mathscr H_{k,\gamma}(P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)})
\right),
\qquad
P_k^{(\gamma)}
=
\mathscr R_k\!\left(
\mathscr H_{k,\gamma}(P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)})
\right).
$$

We prove the claim by backward induction.  At the terminal layer,
$P_{L+1}^{(\gamma)}=P_{L+1}^{\mathrm{LQR}}=Q_f$, so the claim holds.
We first note that every value matrix appearing in either recursion is
positive semidefinite whenever the robust recursion is feasible.  Indeed, if
$P\succeq0$ and
$\gamma^2I-\Dk^\top P\Dk\succ0$, then
$\mathscr H_{k,\gamma}(P)\succeq0$.  Moreover, for any $X\succeq0$,
the block factorization
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
X & X\Bk\\
\Bk^\top X & R_k+\Bk^\top X\Bk
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
I&0\\
\Bk^\top&I
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
X&0\\
0&R_k
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
I&\Bk\\
0&I
\end{bmatrix}
\succeq0
$$
and $R_k+\Bk^\top X\Bk\succ0$ imply, by the Schur complement, that
$$
X-X\Bk
\left(R_k+\Bk^\top X\Bk\right)^{-1}
\Bk^\top X
\succeq0.
$$
Hence $\mathscr R_k(X)\succeq0$.  Starting from $Q_f\succeq0$, backward
induction therefore gives
$P_k^{(\gamma)}\succeq0$ and $P_k^{\mathrm{LQR}}\succeq0$ at every
well-defined layer.

Assume that, for some $k<L+1$,
$$
\norm{P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}-P_{k+1}^{\mathrm{LQR}}}_2
\leq
C_{k+1}\gamma^{-2}
$$
for all sufficiently large $\gamma$.  In particular,
$P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}$ is uniformly bounded: there is a constant
$\bar p_{k+1}$
such that
$\norm{P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}}_2\leq\bar p_{k+1}$.
Consequently,
$$
\norm{
\Dk^\top P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}\Dk
}_2
\leq
\norm{\Dk}_2^2\bar p_{k+1}
=:
d_k.
$$
Choose $\gamma$ large enough that $\gamma^2\geq2d_k$.  Then
$$
\gamma^2I-\Dk^\top P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}\Dk
\succeq
\frac{\gamma^2}{2}I,
$$
so the disturbance Hessian is positive definite and
$$
\norm{
\left(
\gamma^2I-\Dk^\top P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}\Dk
\right)^{-1}
}_2
\leq
\frac{2}{\gamma^2}.
$$
Using the definition of $\mathscr H_{k,\gamma}$ and submultiplicativity of the
spectral norm,
$$
\norm{
\mathscr H_{k,\gamma}(P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)})
-P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}
}_2
\leq
\norm{P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}}_2^2
\norm{\Dk}_2^2
\norm{
\left(
\gamma^2I-\Dk^\top P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)}\Dk
\right)^{-1}
}_2
\leq
\frac{
2\bar p_{k+1}^2\norm{\Dk}_2^2
}{\gamma^2}.
$$
Combining this estimate with the induction hypothesis gives
$$
\norm{
\mathscr H_{k,\gamma}(P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)})
-P_{k+1}^{\mathrm{LQR}}
}_2
=
\mathcal O(\gamma^{-2}).
$$

Because $R_k\succeq rI$ and all value matrices above are positive
semidefinite,
$$
\norm{
\left(R_k+\Bk^\top X\Bk\right)^{-1}
}_2
\leq
r^{-1}.
$$
For completeness, we now make the required Lipschitz constants explicit.
Let
$$
S_k(X)=R_k+\Bk^\top X\Bk,
$$
and take $m_k<\infty$ such that $\norm{X}_2,\norm{Y}_2\leq m_k$ on a bounded
set containing $P_{k+1}^{\mathrm{LQR}}$ and
$\mathscr H_{k,\gamma}(P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)})$ for all sufficiently large
$\gamma$. For $X,Y\succeq0$, the inverse-difference identity gives
$$
S_k(X)^{-1}-S_k(Y)^{-1}
=
S_k(X)^{-1}\Bk^\top(Y-X)\Bk S_k(Y)^{-1},
$$
and therefore
$$
\norm{S_k(X)^{-1}-S_k(Y)^{-1}}_2
\leq
r^{-2}\norm{\Bk}_2^2\norm{X-Y}_2.
$$
Writing $a_k=\norm{\Ak}_2$ and $b_k=\norm{\Bk}_2$, it follows directly that
$$
\norm{\mathscr K_k(X)-\mathscr K_k(Y)}_2
\leq
L_k^K\norm{X-Y}_2,
\qquad
L_k^K
=
a_kb_k\left(r^{-1}+r^{-2}b_k^2m_k\right).
$$
Moreover,
$\norm{\mathscr K_k(X)}_2\leq r^{-1}a_kb_km_k$, and the identity
$$
\mathscr R_k(X)-\mathscr R_k(Y)
=
\Ak^\top(X-Y)\Ak
-\Ak^\top(X-Y)\Bk\mathscr K_k(X)
-\Ak^\top Y\Bk
\left[\mathscr K_k(X)-\mathscr K_k(Y)\right]
$$
implies
$$
\norm{\mathscr R_k(X)-\mathscr R_k(Y)}_2
\leq
L_k^P\norm{X-Y}_2,
$$
where, for example, one may take
$$
L_k^P
=
a_k^2
+a_kb_km_k
\left(r^{-1}a_kb_k+L_k^K\right).
$$
These constants are finite and independent of $\gamma$. Applying these two
bounds with
$X=\mathscr H_{k,\gamma}(P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)})$ and
$Y=P_{k+1}^{\mathrm{LQR}}$ yields
$$
\norm{\Kk^{(\gamma)}-\Kk^{\mathrm{LQR}}}_2
\leq
L_k^K
\norm{
\mathscr H_{k,\gamma}(P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)})
-P_{k+1}^{\mathrm{LQR}}
}_2
=
\mathcal O(\gamma^{-2}),
$$
and
$$
\norm{P_k^{(\gamma)}-P_k^{\mathrm{LQR}}}_2
\leq
L_k^P
\norm{
\mathscr H_{k,\gamma}(P_{k+1}^{(\gamma)})
-P_{k+1}^{\mathrm{LQR}}
}_2
=
\mathcal O(\gamma^{-2}).
$$
This closes the induction.  Since the horizon is finite, one may take the
maximum of the finitely many layerwise feasibility thresholds and constants,
which proves both eventual feasibility and the stated convergence at every
layer.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite-horizon entropy interpretation}

Let $\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}$ be the lifted finite-horizon map from the
stacked disturbance to the regulated output.  For
$\gamma>\norm{\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}}_2$, define
$$
\mathcal{E}_\gamma
\left(\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}\right)
=
-\gamma^2
\log\det\left(
I-\gamma^{-2}
\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}^\top
\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}
\right).
$$
If $\{\sigma_i\}$ are the singular values of
$\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}$, then
$$
\mathcal{E}_\gamma
=
\sum_i
-\gamma^2\log\left(1-\frac{\sigma_i^2}{\gamma^2}\right)
=
\norm{\mathcal{T}_{\steervar{K}}}_F^2
+
\frac{1}{2\gamma^2}\sum_i\sigma_i^4
+
\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{-4}).
$$
Thus, at large $\gamma$, the entropy reduces to the finite-horizon
$\mathrm{H}_2$ energy.  As $\gamma$ approaches the largest singular value,
the contribution of the worst amplified direction diverges.  This provides a
spectral interpretation of the continuation: decreasing $\gamma$ gradually
shifts emphasis from aggregate disturbance energy toward the most damaging
disturbance direction.  The classical minimum-entropy controller makes this
connection precise for standard $\mathrm{H}_{\infty}$ control problems
\citep{doyle1989state,iglesias1990discrete}.

\subsection{Integral augmentation}
\label{app:integral-steering}

Let
$\tilde{x}_k=\operatorname{col}(x_k,i_k)$ and define
$$
\tilde A_k
=
\begin{bmatrix}
A_k&0\\C_{\mathrm{tar}}&\lambda_I I
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\tilde B_k
=
\begin{bmatrix}B_k\\0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\tilde D_k
=
\begin{bmatrix}D_k\\0\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Choosing, for example,
$\tilde Q_k=\operatorname{blkdiag}(Q_k,Q_k^I)$ and applying the original
backward recursion to
$(\tilde A_k,\tilde B_k,\tilde D_k,\tilde Q_k,R_k)$ yields an augmented
controller and the same finite-horizon disturbance-attenuation certificate.
When $\lambda_I=1$,
$i_k=\sum_{j<k}C_{\mathrm{tar}}x_j$ records cumulative semantic error; choosing
$\lambda_I<1$ discounts early-layer errors and limits finite-horizon windup.

For an infinite-horizon, time-invariant system with a constant reference, the
usual zero-offset statement requires additional assumptions: if the augmented
closed loop is asymptotically stable and $i_k$ converges, then
$C_{\mathrm{tar}}x_k=i_{k+1}-i_k\rightarrow0$.  These assumptions do not hold
literally for a finite transformer depth.  Accordingly, our experiments test
whether integral augmentation reduces persistent layerwise bias; they do not
claim asymptotic zero tracking error for the transformer.
