\section{Datasets, Evaluation Protocols, and Full Results}
\label{app:datasets}
\label{app:full-results}

This appendix documents each evaluation suite and reports the corresponding
full results.  All controllers are calibrated once and frozen before
evaluation: no held-out language, jailbreak template, translated prompt, or
long-context trajectory is used to identify the dynamics, estimate the
disturbance channel, select controller gains, or tune intervention strength.
IR, fluency, and task-specific relevance use fixed prompts and the same judge
configuration for every method.  The camera-ready version should specify the
judge model, prompt-template identifiers, decoding parameters, and parsing
rules here: \emph{[insert judge configuration]}.  Unless stated otherwise,
bold denotes the best steering method for a fixed model and condition.

\paragraph{Steering--evaluation alignment.}
We distinguish two experimental regimes.  In \emph{evaluation-aligned}
settings, the principal benchmark outcome directly measures the behavior being
steered: truthfulness on TruthfulQA and refusal success on HarmBench.  Success
therefore requires improving the target outcome while avoiding degenerate or
low-quality generations.  In \emph{evaluation-orthogonal} settings, the
steered attribute is distinct from the benchmark capability: we steer the
output language on MGSM and sentiment on L-CiteEval, while mathematical
accuracy, answer recall, and citation quality should be preserved.  Success in
this regime requires both target attainment and capability retention.  Here,
``orthogonal'' describes the roles of the evaluation objectives; it does not
assert geometric orthogonality between their representation directions.

\subsection{TruthfulQA: Prompt-Distribution Shift}
\label{app:truthfulqa-full}

TruthfulQA contains 817 questions designed to elicit common misconceptions
and imitative falsehoods \citep{lin2022truthfulqa}.  We use English prompts for
matched evaluation and a Spanish-input, English-output variant for shift.  The
controller is calibrated only in English.  We report truthful and informative
responses, instruction relevance (IR), and fluency (F), with the latter two on
a $0$--$2$ scale.  Quality-adjusted truthfulness is
$\mathrm{QAT}=\mathrm{True}(\%)\min\{\mathrm{IR}/2,\mathrm{F}/2\}$.
Results are means over five repetitions; uncertainty is the standard error.
The original informativeness score is retained for completeness but excluded
from QAT because manual inspection found it unstable across steering methods.

\paragraph{Analysis.}
Table~\ref{tab:truthfulqa-full} shows that, on Gemma-2-2B,
$\mathrm{H}_{\infty}$ improves the joint
truthfulness--quality trade-off in both regimes: relative to A-LQR, QAT rises
from $35.81$ to $37.68$ ID and from $39.12$ to $39.50$ OOD.  On Llama-3-8B,
the robust controller produces the largest truthfulness increase but sacrifices
quality under matched evaluation; under Spanish-to-English transfer it instead
achieves the best QAT ($41.33$).  This distinction supports our central claim:
robust synthesis is most useful when deployment dynamics depart from
calibration, rather than as a uniformly superior nominal controller.  The
Qwen2.5-14B and Qwen2.5-32B blocks will test whether this pattern persists with
model scale.

\subsection{HarmBench: Adversarial Prompt Shift}
\label{app:harmbench-full}

HarmBench standardizes adversarial red teaming and robust-refusal evaluation
\citep{mazeika2024harmbench}.  We apply five frozen human-jailbreak templates
to the same 240 behavior identities used for direct requests: John persona,
DNE nonresponse, Jailbreak Bot, YOJA/Nona roleplay, and APM programmer.  Attack
success rate (ASR; lower is better) is the primary safety endpoint.  We also
report safe-concept relevance, instruction relevance, fluency, and aggregate
steering quality on a $0$--$2$ scale.  Safe-concept relevance tests whether low
ASR reflects the intended refusal behavior.  The remaining judgments are
reported for transparency but are not treated as safety objectives because a
successful refusal should not comply with an unsafe instruction.

\paragraph{Analysis.}
Table~\ref{tab:lciteeval-full} shows that the matched $8$K evaluation does not
favor robust synthesis: S-PID has the
highest answer recall, while A-LQR has the largest controlled OS.  After the
shift to $16$K, A-LQR's CF1 falls from $5.6$ to $3.0$ and S-PID's AR falls
from $72.1\%$ to $61.9\%$.  In contrast, $\mathrm{H}_{\infty}$ preserves AR
($66.4\%\!\rightarrow\!65.2\%$) and improves CF1 from $6.4$ to $7.8$, giving
the best CF1 and OS among controlled methods.  The pending $32$K evaluation
and two additional model families reserved in
Table~\ref{tab:lciteeval-future-models} test whether this robustness crossover
strengthens with shift severity and persists across architectures.
